# Small (<1B) Models/Approaches for High-Faithfulness Grounded Rewriting

## Are there small (<1B) models specifically fine-tuned/evaluated for high-faithfulness summarization/rewriting, as opposed to general instruction-tuned checkpoints?

### Takeaway
Dedicated faithfulness benchmarking of sub-1B models is sparse and scattered rather than a mature leaderboard practice — the major hallucination leaderboards (e.g. Vectara's) do not include sub-1B checkpoints at all, and the handful of papers that do test small models use ad hoc model sets. The most directly relevant findings come from one 2025 small-model summarization study and from classic summarization-specific architectures (Pegasus, Brio), not from the new wave of small instruction-tuned chat models (SmolLM, Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B, Phi-3-mini, Gemma) being evaluated for factuality as a primary axis.

### Cited Findings
- The Vectara hallucination leaderboard (HHEM-2.3-based, summarization task) contains no models under ~3B parameters; the smallest entries are Ministral-3B, Gemma-3-4B-it, Qwen3-4B and Phi-4-mini-instruct (~3.8B), so T5/BART/Pegasus-scale or sub-1B chat models are simply absent from this widely-cited public benchmark — [Vectara hallucination-leaderboard](https://github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard/)
- FactCC (Kryscinski et al.) is a weakly-supervised, model-based factual-consistency classifier, and a large-scale human evaluation found it correlates well with human judgments on CNN/DM and XSum — cited via [FRSUM paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.00294) (Pagnoni et al. 2021 finding)
- SummaC (Laban et al. 2021) is described as achieving state-of-the-art performance on summary-inconsistency detection, and FRSUM (a seq2seq faithfulness-robustness training method evaluated with T5/BART-style backbones) uses both FactCC and SummaC — [FRSUM: Towards Faithful Abstractive Summarization via Enhancing Factual Robustness](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.00294)
- A 2023 EMNLP paper (Jia et al.) built a faithfulness-evaluation metric on a "moderately-sized" foundation LM and reports it performs competitively with, or outperforms, ChatGPT on inconsistency detection and faithfulness rating with 24x fewer parameters — this is a faithfulness *judge*, not a generator, but shows moderate-size models can be competitive evaluators — [Zero-shot Faithfulness Evaluation for Text Summarization with Foundation Language Model](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2023.emnlp-main.679)
- "TrueBrief: Faithful Summarization through Small Language Models" (arXiv, submitted Dec 2025) exists and targets exactly this problem — a preference-optimization approach where a data-generation module deliberately injects hallucinations to build synthetic preference data for training small LMs to be more faithful — but the abstract alone does not specify model sizes/architectures, the benchmarks used, or quantitative results; the question of "how faithful, by how much, vs what baseline" is unresolved from available text — [TrueBrief](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04212v1)
- A 2025 study, "Evaluating Small Language Models for News Summarization," directly benchmarks sub-1B/near-1B models for factual consistency using HHEM-2.1-Open (a fine-tuned-T5-based hallucination detector) across XSum, Newsroom, CNN/DM, and a 2024 BBC dataset. Reported average factual-consistency rates: **Pegasus-Large (568M): 99.9%**, **Brio (406M, a summarization-specialized model): 94.3%**, **Qwen2-0.5B (base, no instruction tuning): 94.5%**, **Qwen2-0.5B-Instruct: 89.0%**, **Bloom-560M: 82.9%**, **LiteLlama (460M): 64.1%** — [Evaluating Small Language Models for News Summarization: Implications and Factors Influencing Performance](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00641)
- The same paper found instruction tuning *reduced* factual consistency in this size class: Qwen2-0.5B-Instruct (89.0% avg) scored below base Qwen2-0.5B (94.5% avg), and the paper states "models without instruction tuning often exhibit higher factual consistency" — [Evaluating Small Language Models for News Summarization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00641)
- The same paper found the instruction-tuned small model was highly sensitive to prompt complexity — Qwen2-0.5B-Ins consistency dropped from 89.6% to 78.2% when moving from a simple to a more complex prompt — and concludes very small models "are not well-suited for complex prompt design" — [Evaluating Small Language Models for News Summarization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00641)
- The paper attributes Pegasus-Large's top score to an extractive-leaning behavior: it "often copies sentences from the original text, ensuring alignment but sometimes missing key information" — i.e., its faithfulness comes partly from being conservative/copy-heavy, a tradeoff against informativeness/completeness, which is directly relevant to a "preserve all facts" requirement (copying is safe for precision but risks omission/recall) — [Evaluating Small Language Models for News Summarization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00641)
- FaithBench (a hallucination benchmark of summaries from "modern LLMs") ranks Phi-3-mini-4k 8th of 10 models with a 63.93% "unwanted hallucination" rate — but this model is larger (~3.8B) than the sub-1B class and the benchmark's scoring scheme differs from HHEM-based rates, so it is not directly comparable to the numbers above — [FaithBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.13210)
- A separate news-summarization factuality study found Phi3-Mini achieved 96.7% average factual consistency (>95% on all datasets), and its Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct scored 92.5% — again a larger/different model set than strict sub-1B, and seemingly in tension with FaithBench's ranking of Phi-3-mini, underscoring that these numbers are highly benchmark/methodology-dependent and should not be cross-compared — source surfaced only as a search snippet; original paper not independently re-verified in this pass (see Gaps)
- No evaluation was found that tests SmolLM/SmolLM2 as a *generator* for summarization faithfulness; the only SmolLM-related hit was SmolLM2-135M-Instruct used as a hallucination *detector* (F1 0.62) in a W&B project, not as the model being evaluated for faithful output — [Hallucination Scorer report](https://wandb.ai/c-metrics/hallucination/reports/Hallucination-Scorer--VmlldzoxMDM3NDA3MA)

### Inferences
- The field has clearly separated "faithfulness evaluators" (where moderate/small models are a validated, active area — FactCC, SummaC, AlignScore, TrueTeacher-trained students) from "faithful generators at small scale," which is comparatively under-benchmarked. Treat any specific generator numbers as provisional/benchmark-specific rather than settled.
- The one concrete sub-1B generator comparison available (Qwen2-0.5B base vs. -Instruct) suggests that generic instruction-tuning — the kind flan-t5-small also received — can actively *hurt* narrow-task factual faithfulness relative to a less "chatty," more literal base/task-specific model. This directly supports investigating a non-generic-instruction-tuned alternative.
- Summarization-specialized architectures trained to be more extractive/copy-biased (Pegasus, Brio) outperform general small instruction-following models on faithfulness, but likely at a cost to the "combine distinct facts into one fluent sentence" requirement, since copying does not inherently fuse multiple facts.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that runs a controlled, apples-to-apples comparison of flan-t5-small itself against other sub-1B checkpoints on a standard faithfulness benchmark (SummaC/FactCC/AlignScore) for the specific "rewrite verified facts into prose" task.
- TrueBrief's full methodology, model sizes, and quantitative results could not be retrieved (only the abstract was accessible); this is the single most relevant paper found and warrants a follow-up fetch of the full PDF/HTML if deeper detail is needed.
- No source gives DistilBART or Pegasus-small (as opposed to Pegasus-Large) faithfulness numbers specifically; searches turned up only adjacent LLM-evaluation papers using distilBART/distil-PEGASUS as baselines with unclear metric definitions.
- The Phi3-Mini 96.7%-consistency claim comes from a search-engine summary that could not be traced to a verified primary source in this pass; it should be treated as unconfirmed pending direct retrieval of that paper.

## Does fine-tuning a small seq2seq model on a narrow task-specific dataset (e.g., "combine N short facts into one sentence") produce more reliable content preservation than off-the-shelf zero/few-shot instruction-tuned use? Are there published datasets/recipes resembling this?

### Takeaway
There is no paper found that tests this exact framing ("combine N facts, preserve all") head-to-head, but closely analogous evidence (sentence fusion, narrow factual-classification/consistency tasks) consistently shows fine-tuned small/specialized models matching or beating zero-shot prompting of larger instruction-tuned models on narrow, well-defined tasks — provided labeled/constructed training data exists. The clearest directly-relevant published dataset for "combine inputs into one fused sentence while preserving content" is DiscoFuse (sentence fusion), not a generic summarization corpus.

### Cited Findings
- DiscoFuse is a large-scale (60M example) dataset built specifically for sentence fusion: discourse-aware rules split real sentences into two independent ones, and the *original* (unsplit) sentence is the fusion target — meaning examples are constructed so the target provably contains all the content of the inputs by construction — [DiscoFuse: A Large-Scale Dataset for Discourse-Based Sentence Fusion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.10526)
- DiscoFuse was explicitly designed so a model cannot merely learn to invert the splitting rule, because some information (e.g., discourse connectives like "however") is lost in the split and must be inferred from meaning — meaning it also trains for fluent reconstruction, not just concatenation — [DiscoFuse](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.10526)
- Pretraining on DiscoFuse substantially improved performance on a smaller downstream fusion dataset (WebSplit) in transfer experiments, i.e., pretraining on a large synthetic fusion corpus then adapting to a smaller task-specific one is a documented, working recipe for this task family — [DiscoFuse](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.10526)
- DiscoFuse's own evaluation uses SARI and human judgment, not explicit per-fact recall/precision — so even this close analogue does not report a "fraction of input facts preserved" metric; that metric would need to be added — [DiscoFuse](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.10526)
- In a related narrow factual task (classifying/detecting factual inconsistency in summaries), zero-shot LLM prompting beat strong *trained* baselines by about 2.8% on average — but the same paper then distilled a smaller open-source model (trained on LLM-generated labels) that outperformed zero-shot prompting of much larger LLMs, concluding the task is "train-free" solvable at LLM scale but that distilling a small trained model for practical deployment surpasses the zero-shot LLM baseline — [Identifying Factual Inconsistency in Summaries](https://www.catalyzex.com/paper/identifying-factual-inconsistency-in) (details via search synopsis; original full text not independently fetched — see Gaps)
- In an unrelated but structurally similar narrow-classification comparison (misinformation-discourse labeling on 900 Reddit comments), a fine-tuned RoBERTa reached 0.62 macro-F1 versus a best zero-shot LLM result of 0.50 (Claude Haiku 4.5), at much lower per-query cost — illustrating the general pattern that fine-tuned small specialized models can beat zero-shot prompting of larger instruction models on narrow, labeled tasks — source surfaced via search synopsis only, title/venue not fully confirmed (see Gaps)
- TrueTeacher demonstrates the "distill a small faithful specialist from an LLM teacher" recipe concretely for factual-consistency *evaluation*: an LLM labels diverse model-generated summaries for factual consistency, producing 1.4M synthetic training examples; the resulting *student* model "substantially outperforms both the state-of-the-art model with similar capacity, and the LLM teacher" — a checkpoint and dataset were publicly released — [TrueTeacher: Learning Factual Consistency Evaluation with Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.11171)
- LIM-RA shows that curating/cleaning a much smaller training set (~10% the size) for a DeBERTa-based alignment/faithfulness model outperforms the original (heuristically-constructed, noisier) AlignScore training data and beats ChatGPT across four benchmarks — evidence that data *quality* and task-specificity of fine-tuning data matters more than sheer volume for this family of narrow tasks — source surfaced via search synopsis (see Gaps for primary-source caveat)

### Inferences
- The strongest documented recipe transferable to "combine verified facts into one sentence" is: (1) construct or synthesize a dataset where the target output is guaranteed-by-construction to contain all source content (as DiscoFuse does via reverse-engineering real sentence splits), (2) optionally pretrain on a large synthetic corpus of this kind, then (3) fine-tune/adapt on a smaller in-domain set matching the exact target task.
- Across the (admittedly adjacent, not identical) narrow-task comparisons found, the pattern "fine-tuned small specialist ≥ zero-shot larger generalist" recurs for factual/consistency-adjacent classification tasks whenever labeled or LLM-distilled training data is available. No counter-example was found where zero-shot a small instruction-tuned generalist model beat a fine-tuned specialist on a narrow, well-defined factual task.
- None of the fusion/faithfulness datasets found report a "percent of input facts retained" metric directly; a practitioner adopting this approach would need to add a custom evaluation (e.g., per-fact entailment or QA-based recall checking) since existing benchmarks (SARI, BLEU, ROUGE) do not directly measure fact-dropping.

### Gaps
- No paper tests the literal framing "combine N short verified facts into one fluent sentence, preserving all of them" as a fine-tuning task with a fact-recall metric; DiscoFuse-style sentence fusion is the closest available proxy but operates on naturally-split sentence pairs, not a curated list of discrete facts.
- Two findings above (the Reddit misinformation fine-tuning comparison, and the "Identifying Factual Inconsistency" distillation paper) were retrieved only via search-result synopses; their primary sources were not independently fetched and verified in this research pass, so treat the specific numbers as secondhand pending verification.
- No published recipe was found for "LoRA/PEFT fine-tuning specifically for fact-preservation in short-input rewriting" — the adjacent PEFT evidence found concerns faithfulness in open-ended summarization, not fusion/combination of a fixed small fact set (see next question).

## What does data-to-text generation research say about small models' reliability at faithfully verbalizing a fixed set of structured facts?

### Takeaway
Data-to-text (D2T) research is the closest existing subfield to "combine known facts into prose," and it has long treated hallucination/omission as a first-class, well-studied failure mode distinct from summarization research — but even here, small models (T5-Small specifically) are shown in primary sources to produce measurably lower content-fidelity (PARENT/BLEU) than larger siblings, and the field explicitly flags that fluent, well-formed output is not a guarantee of faithfulness.

### Cited Findings
- The foundational T5-for-D2T study (Kale & Rastogi, "Text-to-Text Pre-Training for Data-to-Text Tasks") reports T5-Small at **52.0 BLEU** overall on WebNLG (62.6 on seen categories, 38.8 on unseen), with 0.41 METEOR — these are substantially below larger T5 variants in the same paper's table (exact T5-Base/Large/3B deltas not restated here, but the seen/unseen split shows T5-Small struggles much more on unseen predicate/category generalization, 62.6 vs 38.8) — [Text-to-Text Pre-Training for Data-to-Text Tasks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10433)
- The same paper reports T5-Small on ToTTo dev set at **45.7 BLEU / 55.9 PARENT** overall, and **37.7 BLEU / 51.6 PARENT** on the non-overlap subset (tables compare T5-Small/Base/Large/3B) — PARENT is specifically a table-to-text metric designed to penalize hallucinated/unsupported content and reward faithful coverage of the source table — [Text-to-Text Pre-Training for Data-to-Text Tasks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10433)
- The D2T literature explicitly names two distinct failure types relevant to "preserve all facts": hallucinations (injecting content unrelated to input data) and simple factual errors (coherent but factually wrong output) — and names WebNLG and ToTTo as the field's two main public benchmarks — [Faithful Low-Resource Data-to-Text Generation through Cycle Training](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14793)
- Cycle training (pairing a D2T generator with a text-to-data reconstructor, using only a pretrained T5 with no graph-specific machinery) can reach near fully-supervised performance on WebNLG, E2E, WTQ and WSQL starting from just 100 labeled examples — directly relevant as a low-label-cost route to improving faithfulness, since the reconstruction loss implicitly penalizes dropped/invented facts — [Faithful Low-Resource Data-to-Text Generation through Cycle Training](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14793)
- A Cambridge MPhil dissertation approach trains a T5 generator on a cleaned/mixed corpus, generates 5 candidates per input, and uses a separate RoBERTa "fidelity classifier" to select the most faithful candidate; this candidate-filtering step raised METEOR by 4.72% (vs. 1.19% in a prior comparable method), illustrating that a lightweight post-hoc faithfulness reranker/filter on top of a small generator is a documented, tractable way to raise measured faithfulness without changing the base model's training — [dissertation PDF](https://www.mlmi.eng.cam.ac.uk/files/2020-2021_dissertations/controlling_hallucination_while_generating_text.pdf)
- Confidence-based decoding-time correction (Tian et al. 2019) treats hallucination as the decoder generating content without properly attending to the source, and proposes a confidence score correction that improves PARENT and human-eval faithfulness on WikiBio and WebNLG — an inference-time (no-retraining) mitigation applicable to small encoder-decoder models — [Sticking to the Facts: Confident Decoding for Faithful Data-to-Text Generation](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.08684)
- The UFACT line of work (Findings of ACL 2022) argues state-of-the-art WebNLG results (by METEOR, with PARENT baselines reported) can be reached even when training data itself contains substantial "unfaithful" examples, implying faithful *generation* behavior can be partly decoupled from needing a perfectly clean training corpus — [UFACT paper](https://aclweb.org/anthology/2022.findings-acl.223.pdf)

### Inferences
- D2T research gives a more direct and mature toolkit than general summarization research for the "combine known facts" task: (a) PARENT as a metric purpose-built to score faithfulness/coverage against a structured input, (b) cycle-training as a low-label faithfulness-boosting training recipe, and (c) candidate generation + fidelity-reranking as a cheap post-hoc faithfulness improvement — all demonstrated specifically on T5-family small models.
- Even within this more favorable subfield, raw T5-Small underperforms larger T5 variants on faithfulness-sensitive metrics (PARENT) and especially struggles to generalize faithfully to unseen predicate/category combinations (WebNLG unseen-category BLEU drops nearly in half vs. seen). This suggests scale still helps even on narrow D2T tasks, but the gap can plausibly be narrowed by task-specific fine-tuning, cycle training, or reranking — none of which require abandoning a sub-1B model.

### Gaps
- No source found gives a direct, controlled comparison of T5-Small fine-tuned on D2T versus flan-t5-small used zero-shot/few-shot on an equivalent "verbalize these facts" prompt — the D2T literature evaluates fine-tuned checkpoints throughout, not prompted off-the-shelf instruction models, so a like-for-like "fine-tune vs. just prompt" comparison specific to D2T was not located.
- Exact T5-Base/Large/3B comparison numbers from the Kale & Rastogi paper (to quantify precisely how much scale buys on PARENT/BLEU) were not fully re-extracted in this pass beyond what's cited above; a follow-up fetch of the full table would sharpen this.

## Are lightweight fine-tuning techniques (LoRA, prompt-tuning, etc.) documented as sufficient to meaningfully improve a small T5 model's faithfulness on a narrow rewrite task?

### Takeaway
LoRA/PEFT is well-documented as a mechanically easy way to adapt flan-t5-small to a summarization-style task cheaply (training a tiny fraction of parameters), but no source found specifically measures whether LoRA/PEFT (as opposed to full fine-tuning) changes *faithfulness* outcomes on a narrow task — the faithfulness-focused fine-tuning literature (FRSUM, span-level unlikelihood training) uses full fine-tuning or LoRA at much larger scale, not small T5 models, leaving this a documented gap rather than a documented positive or negative result.

### Cited Findings
- A practical tutorial fine-tunes flan-t5-small with LoRA for summarization, noting LoRA avoids updating all 77M parameters of the base model — confirming mechanical/computational feasibility of LoRA on exactly this checkpoint — [DEV Community LoRA summarization tutorial](https://dev.to/sri_harikarthik_909342ac/fine-tuning-a-language-model-for-summarisation-using-lora-3lg)
- A published Hugging Face LoRA adapter for flan-t5-small (dialogue summarization) trains only 0.16% of parameters, but is explicitly *not* evaluated for faithfulness, and its own model card warns users to monitor for hallucination in outputs — i.e., an existing real-world small-LoRA artifact exists but ships with an open, unresolved faithfulness caveat rather than a resolved one — [flan-t5-small-summary-peft model card](https://huggingface.co/gnokit/flan-t5-small-summary-peft)
- FRSUM (a method that improves faithfulness by training for "factual robustness") reports significant faithfulness gains across Seq2Seq baselines including T5, but its experiments use full fine-tuning, not LoRA/PEFT — [FRSUM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.00294)
- A 2025 span-level fine-tuning paper for hallucination reduction (using span-level hallucination labels with gradient ascent, unlikelihood training, and task-vector negation, finding unlikelihood training most effective) uses LoRA at rank 128 — but on much larger LLMs, not T5-small-scale models, so the specific rank/configuration is not directly transferable evidence for sub-1B models — [Enhancing Faithfulness in Abstractive Summarization via Span-Level Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09915)
- Prompt-tuning's foundational paper (Lester et al.) finds prompt tuning's competitiveness with full model tuning is scale-dependent — it "performs considerably closer to the strong performance of model tuning" mainly as model size grows, implying the gap between prompt-tuning and full fine-tuning is typically *larger*, not smaller, at small-model scale (i.e., prompt tuning is less reliable exactly at the sub-1B sizes relevant here) — [The Power of Scale for Parameter-Efficient Prompt Tuning](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.08691)
- No paper was found applying prefix- or prompt-tuning specifically to a small encoder-decoder (T5/BART-scale) model and measuring faithfulness/hallucination as the outcome; the one factual-consistency-linked prefix-tuning result found (Chen, Zhang & Shakeri 2023) uses GPT-2, a decoder-only model, with discrete OpenIE-triple prompts, and reports factually-consistent-summary improvements on CNN/DM and XSum, but is not a T5-family seq2seq result — [self-consistent narrative prompts context](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.08303) and search-derived description of Chen/Zhang/Shakeri 2023 (primary paper not independently fetched — see Gaps)

### Inferences
- Mechanically, LoRA/PEFT on flan-t5-small for a narrow rewrite task is trivial and well-trodden (multiple working tutorials/adapters exist). The open question is not feasibility but whether PEFT captures enough of the faithfulness-improving signal that full fine-tuning would — this has simply not been measured in available literature for this size class.
- Given that prompt-tuning's main advantage (near-parity with full fine-tuning) is explicitly reported as a function of scale, and flan-t5-small sits near the bottom of model sizes typically studied, LoRA (which updates actual weight deltas, not just an input embedding) is likely the safer choice over pure prompt/prefix-tuning for a sub-1B model if faithfulness gains are the goal — but this is an inference, not a directly cited experimental result.

### Gaps
- No controlled ablation (LoRA vs. full fine-tuning vs. prompt-tuning, all on T5-small/flan-t5-small, measured on a faithfulness metric like AlignScore/PARENT/entity-recall) was found anywhere in this search. This is the single clearest open gap for anyone deciding how to fine-tune flan-t5-small for faithfulness — it would likely require either a targeted literature deep-dive beyond this pass or running the ablation directly.
- The Chen/Zhang/Shakeri (2023) prefix-tuning-for-factual-consistency paper's full details (exact metrics, magnitude of improvement) were not independently verified from primary source in this pass.

## Is there a case for "fine-tune flan-t5-small (or similar) on a custom narrow dataset for this rewrite task" vs. "this is inherently unreliable at this scale regardless of fine-tuning"?

### Takeaway
The available evidence leans toward "narrow fine-tuning is a well-documented, tractable, and probably necessary fix" rather than "inherently unreliable at this scale" — multiple analogous narrow-task results show fine-tuned small/specialized models matching or beating zero-shot larger instruction models, and D2T research shows small T5 models can be pushed toward much higher faithfulness with task-specific training/cycle-training/reranking — but no source directly proves this for flan-t5-small on exactly the user's task, so the case is built from strong analogy rather than a direct demonstration.

### Cited Findings
- Generic instruction-tuning measurably reduced narrow-task factual consistency in the one sub-1B head-to-head found (Qwen2-0.5B 94.5% avg factual consistency vs. Qwen2-0.5B-Instruct 89.0% avg, dropping further to 78.2% under more complex prompts) — this is direct evidence that an off-the-shelf general instruction-tuned checkpoint (the flan-t5-small category) is not obviously the most faithful option even relative to its own non-instruction-tuned base, let alone a task-specifically fine-tuned model — [Evaluating Small Language Models for News Summarization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00641)
- Summarization-specialized (non-chat-instruction-tuned) architectures — Pegasus-Large (99.9% avg factual consistency) and Brio (94.3%), both sub-600M params — substantially outperformed general small instruction-following models on measured faithfulness, suggesting task/architecture specialization, not raw scale, drives faithfulness gains at this size — [Evaluating Small Language Models for News Summarization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00641)
- In D2T research, cycle-training (pure T5, no extra machinery) reaches near-fully-supervised faithfulness/performance on WebNLG from just 100 labeled examples — showing meaningful faithfulness improvement via fine-tuning is achievable with very modest new data/engineering investment, not a massive dataset build — [Cycle Training paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.14793)
- A lightweight candidate-generation-plus-fidelity-reranking approach (generate 5 candidates from a T5 generator, filter with a separately trained fidelity classifier) raised measured faithfulness (METEOR +4.72%) without retraining the base generator at all — a second, even lower-investment tractable path if full/narrow fine-tuning is judged too costly — [dissertation](https://www.mlmi.eng.cam.ac.uk/files/2020-2021_dissertations/controlling_hallucination_while_generating_text.pdf)
- DiscoFuse demonstrates that a large, cheaply-constructed-by-rule synthetic dataset (derived by splitting real sentences, using the original as the faithful fusion target) can pretrain fusion-style models with guaranteed content coverage — directly transferable as a recipe for building a "combine N facts" dataset from existing fluent sentences/paragraphs (via automatic decomposition) rather than requiring expensive manual annotation — [DiscoFuse](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.10526)
- Conversely, even fine-tuned small T5 models show a real scale-related ceiling in D2T: T5-Small's WebNLG BLEU drops from 62.6 (seen categories) to 38.8 (unseen categories), nearly halving — indicating fine-tuning narrows but does not eliminate a generalization gap, particularly for input compositions unlike anything in training — [Text-to-Text Pre-Training for Data-to-Text Tasks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10433)

### Inferences
- The balance of (admittedly indirect/analogical) evidence supports "fine-tune for this narrow task" over "give up on sub-1B models." The strongest supporting pattern is that every narrow-task comparison found (factual-inconsistency distillation, misinformation classification, sub-1B summarization) favored a fine-tuned/specialized small model over a zero-shot larger-or-equal-size instruction-tuned generalist, and D2T shows concrete, low-data recipes (cycle training, rule-derived synthetic corpora, post-hoc reranking) that specifically target faithfulness in T5-scale models.
- The caveat is that gains from fine-tuning are strongly conditioned on the training distribution matching the deployment distribution — the WebNLG seen/unseen BLEU gap shows fine-tuned small models can still fail badly on fact/predicate combinations unlike their training data. For a production "rewrite verified facts" component, this implies: (a) fine-tuning on data that closely resembles the real fact-tuple distributions expected in deployment is important, and (b) a held-out faithfulness check (e.g., per-fact entailment or an AlignScore/PARENT-style automatic check) should likely gate output in production regardless of fine-tuning, since no evidence found suggests fine-tuning drives failure rates to zero even on in-distribution data (Pegasus-Large's 99.9% is the best found figure, still not 100%).
- No evidence was found for the strong claim "this is inherently unreliable regardless of fine-tuning" — that framing is not supported by anything retrieved; every faithfulness-focused intervention found (training-data design, cycle training, reranking, specialized architecture) produced measurable improvement at this scale.

### Gaps
- The single biggest gap across this whole research task: no paper directly fine-tunes flan-t5-small (or an equivalent small seq2seq model) on a bespoke "merge these N short facts into one faithful sentence" dataset and reports a before/after faithfulness delta. Everything in this report is built from closely analogous but not identical tasks (sentence fusion, D2T verbalization, summarization faithfulness, narrow factual classification). A direct experiment — build a small (hundreds-to-low-thousands example) synthetic dataset in the DiscoFuse style (decompose existing fluent sentences into constituent facts, train the model to reconstruct), fine-tune flan-t5-small with LoRA, and measure with an entailment/fact-recall metric — was not found as already-published work, meaning it is a legitimate, currently-unaddressed empirical contribution rather than a solved problem to merely look up.
